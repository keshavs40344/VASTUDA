What is a Confusion Matrix in Machine Learning?
A Confusion Matrix is a 2x2 table used to evaluate the performance of a binary classification model by comparing actual ground-truth labels against predicted labels. It categorizes outcomes into True Positives (TP), True Negatives (TN), False Positives (FP), and False Negatives (FN).
Why is Accuracy a misleading metric for imbalanced datasets?
Consider a credit card fraud dataset where 99.9% of transactions are legitimate and 0.1% are fraudulent. A naive model that predicts 'Not Fraud' for every single transaction will achieve 99.9% accuracy, yet it catches zero fraud cases! In such scenarios, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score are far more informative.
What is the formula for F1-Score and why is the harmonic mean used?
F1-Score = 2 * (Precision * Recall) / (Precision + Recall). The harmonic mean penalizes extreme values heavily: if either Precision or Recall is close to zero, the F1-Score will drop near zero, unlike an arithmetic average which could conceal a severely imbalanced model.
When should I prioritize Precision over Recall (and vice versa)?
Prioritize Precision when the cost of a False Positive is high (e.g., spam filters where classifying an important email as spam causes lost business). Prioritize Recall when the cost of a False Negative is critical (e.g., cancer detection or airport weapons screening where missing a positive case is life-threatening).
What is Specificity (True Negative Rate)?
Specificity measures the proportion of actual negatives that are correctly identified as negative: Specificity = TN / (TN + FP). It is crucial in clinical diagnostic trials to ensure healthy individuals are not mistakenly diagnosed with a condition.
